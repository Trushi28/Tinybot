# Edit freely. The fact book (/facts) tracks which ones you've heard. Categories: space animals science tech history body math
space: A day on Venus is longer than its year: it spins once every 243 Earth days but orbits the Sun in about 225.
space: Light from the Sun takes about 8 minutes and 20 seconds to reach Earth.
space: Saturn is less dense than water, so in a big enough bathtub it would float.
space: A teaspoon of neutron-star material would weigh billions of tons.
space: Jupiter's Great Red Spot is a storm wider than Earth that has been observed for well over a century.
space: Footprints left on the Moon could last for millions of years because there is no wind or rain to erase them.
space: One solar day on Mercury lasts about 176 Earth days.
space: Voyager 1, launched in 1977, is the most distant human-made object, more than 24 billion kilometres from Earth.
space: There are estimated to be more stars in the observable universe than grains of sand on all of Earth's beaches.
animals: Octopuses have three hearts and blue blood.
animals: A group of flamingos is called a flamboyance.
animals: Sharks are older than trees: they've been around for roughly 450 million years.
animals: Wombats produce cube-shaped droppings.
animals: Crows can recognise individual human faces and remember who was unkind to them.
animals: Honeybees tell each other where flowers are by dancing, the so-called waggle dance.
animals: Asian elephants can recognise themselves in a mirror, which only a few species can do.
animals: Sea otters sometimes hold paws while they sleep so they don't drift apart.
animals: Tardigrades, tiny water bears, have survived exposure to outer space.
animals: Cows form close friendships and get stressed when separated from their best friend.
science: Honey never spoils. Edible honey has been found in ancient Egyptian tombs.
science: Bananas are berries, but strawberries aren't.
science: Lightning is several times hotter than the surface of the Sun.
science: Water expands when it freezes, which is why ice floats.
science: Sound travels about four times faster in water than in air.
science: Diamond and graphite are both made of pure carbon, just arranged differently.
science: Hot water can sometimes freeze faster than cold water, the Mpemba effect, and scientists still argue about why.
science: A single bolt of lightning can briefly heat the air around it to around 30,000 degrees Celsius.
tech: The first computer "bug" was a real moth found in a Harvard Mark II relay in 1947.
tech: Rust's unofficial mascot is a crab named Ferris.
tech: The first message sent over ARPANET in 1969 was meant to be "LOGIN", but the system crashed after "LO".
tech: Ray Tomlinson picked the @ symbol for email addresses in 1971.
tech: Unix time counts the seconds since 1 January 1970 UTC.
tech: Ada Lovelace published what is often called the first algorithm meant for a machine, in the 1840s.
tech: The first webcam pointed at a coffee pot at Cambridge University so researchers could check if it was full.
tech: A kilobyte is 1,000 bytes by SI definition but 1,024 in the older convention, which is why the kibibyte (KiB) exists.
history: Cleopatra lived closer in time to the Moon landing than to the building of the Great Pyramid.
history: Oxford University is older than the Aztec Empire.
history: The Anglo-Zanzibar War of 1896 is considered the shortest war in history, lasting around 40 minutes.
history: Nintendo was founded in 1889 as a playing-card company.
history: The Great Wall of China is not visible to the naked eye from space, despite the popular myth.
history: The Eiffel Tower can grow a few centimetres taller in summer because the iron expands in the heat.
body: Your stomach lining replaces itself every few days.
body: The human brain uses about 20 percent of the body's energy while being around 2 percent of its weight.
body: Babies are born with around 300 bones, which fuse into the 206 adults have.
body: Your heart beats roughly 100,000 times a day.
body: Humans share roughly 98 to 99 percent of their DNA with chimpanzees.
body: The cornea of your eye has no blood supply and gets its oxygen straight from the air.
math: A googol is a 1 followed by 100 zeros, and Google's name is a misspelling of it.
math: A shuffled deck of 52 cards is almost certainly in an order that has never existed before: there are about 8 x 10^67 orderings.
math: Brahmagupta described rules for arithmetic with zero in India in the 7th century.
math: 111,111,111 x 111,111,111 = 12,345,678,987,654,321.
math: Pi has been calculated to over 100 trillion digits.
math: Legend says a young Gauss summed 1 to 100 in seconds by pairing numbers: the answer is 5050.
