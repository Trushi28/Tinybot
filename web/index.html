How it answers

  1. 01

    Skills go first

    Maths, units, dates, dice, timers, notes: exact things are done by exact code, never guessed by a neural net. A safety net for crisis phrases also runs before everything else.

  2. 02

    The network picks an intent

    A bidirectional GRU reads hashed word and character n-grams (so typos still land near their word) and scores 52 intents. The full chat page shows those scores live.

  3. 03

    Context breaks ties

    A 24-turn memory and a learned intent-to-intent prior resolve "and in meters?", "again" and "what did I just say". Unsure? It asks instead of bluffing.

  4. 04

    It remembers you

    Your name, notes, XP, mood and running timers are kept in this browser only. Clear them any time with forget me.

Inside the box

Distilled

A teacher ensemble of 3.2M parameters labels thousands of synthetic phrases; one 251k-parameter student learns to match it. The student ships, with int8 weights.

From scratch

Forward passes, backprop through time, Adam and the int8 file format are all hand-written, with tests that check every gradient against finite differences.

Honest

It is an intent router, not a language model. On phrases it never saw in training it picks the right intent about 81% of the time (about 71% with typos, from 5-fold cross-validation), it says "I don't know" when unsure, and it will still get things wrong.

Runs fully in your browser as WebAssembly: nothing you type is sent anywhere, and notes, XP and timers are saved on this device only. TinyBot is a small hobby model, not a person or a professional. If you are in danger or in crisis, contact your local emergency services or a crisis line.
