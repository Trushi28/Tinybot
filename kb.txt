# ---------------- space
[Solar System | planets planet orbit]
The Solar System has eight planets orbiting the Sun: Mercury, Venus, Earth, Mars, Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus and Neptune.
Pluto is classed as a dwarf planet. The Solar System formed about 4.6 billion years ago.

[Sun | star]
The Sun is the star at the centre of the Solar System and holds about 99.8 percent of its mass.
Its surface is around 5,500 degrees Celsius and it is about 4.6 billion years old.

[Moon | satellite tides lunar]
The Moon is Earth's only natural satellite, about 384,400 kilometres away.
Its gravity causes the ocean tides, and it takes about 27 days to orbit Earth.

[Moon landing | apollo armstrong aldrin]
Apollo 11 landed the first humans on the Moon on 20 July 1969.
Neil Armstrong stepped out first, followed by Buzz Aldrin, while Michael Collins stayed in orbit.

[Earth | home planet]
Earth is the third planet from the Sun and the only place known to have life.
About 71 percent of its surface is water and it is about 4.5 billion years old.

[Mars | red]
Mars is the fourth planet from the Sun, red because of iron oxide in its soil.
It has two small moons, Phobos and Deimos, and Olympus Mons, the tallest volcano in the Solar System at about 22 kilometres.

[Jupiter | gas giant]
Jupiter is the largest planet, a gas giant with more than 1,300 Earths' worth of volume.
Its Great Red Spot is a storm that has lasted for centuries, and it has dozens of moons.

[Saturn | rings]
Saturn is the second largest planet, a gas giant famous for its bright rings made of ice and rock.

[Venus | hottest]
Venus is the hottest planet, about 465 degrees Celsius, because its thick carbon dioxide atmosphere traps heat.
It spins backwards compared with most planets.

[Mercury | closest]
Mercury is the smallest planet and the closest to the Sun. Its year lasts only 88 Earth days.

[Black hole | event horizon singularity]
A black hole is a region of space where gravity is so strong that nothing, not even light, can escape.
Most form when a massive star collapses. The first picture of one was released in 2019.

[Milky Way | galaxy]
The Milky Way is the galaxy that contains the Solar System, a spiral of hundreds of billions of stars.
The Sun is about 26,000 light-years from its centre.

[Big Bang | universe origin age]
The Big Bang is the theory that the universe expanded from an extremely hot, dense state about 13.8 billion years ago.

[Light year | lightyear]
A light-year is the distance light travels in one year, about 9.46 trillion kilometres.

[Speed of light | light fast]
Light travels at about 299,792 kilometres per second in a vacuum, and nothing with mass can reach that speed.

[Gravity | newton einstein]
Gravity is the force that pulls masses toward each other. On Earth it accelerates things at about 9.8 metres per second squared.
Newton described it as a force; Einstein's general relativity describes it as the curving of spacetime.

# ---------------- science
[Photosynthesis | plants chlorophyll chloroplast]
Photosynthesis is how plants, algae and some bacteria use light, water and carbon dioxide to make sugar and oxygen.
It happens in chloroplasts, where chlorophyll absorbs the light. It supplies most of the oxygen we breathe.

[Atom | proton neutron electron]
An atom is the smallest unit of a chemical element: a nucleus of protons and neutrons with electrons around it.

[DNA | gene genetic helix]
DNA is the molecule that carries genetic instructions in living things, shaped like a double helix.
It is written in four bases: A, T, G and C.

[Cell | cells mitochondria]
A cell is the basic unit of life. Mitochondria inside cells produce most of their energy.

[Evolution | darwin natural selection]
Evolution is the change in the inherited traits of populations over generations, driven largely by natural selection.
Charles Darwin published On the Origin of Species in 1859.

[Water cycle | rain evaporation]
The water cycle moves water through evaporation, condensation, precipitation and collection, driven by the Sun.

[Periodic table | elements chemistry mendeleev]
The periodic table arranges the chemical elements by atomic number. There are 118 known elements.
Dmitri Mendeleev published the first widely used version in 1869.

[Electricity | current voltage]
Electricity is the flow of electric charge. Current is measured in amperes and voltage in volts.

[Climate change | global warming greenhouse]
Climate change is the long-term warming of Earth, mostly from burning fossil fuels, which release carbon dioxide and other greenhouse gases.
The planet is about 1.1 degrees Celsius warmer than before the industrial era.

[Quantum mechanics | quantum entanglement physics]
Quantum mechanics is the physics of atoms and light, where particles behave like waves and outcomes are probabilities.
Entanglement links two particles so that measuring one is correlated with the other, but it cannot be used to send messages faster than light.

[Vaccine | vaccination immunity]
A vaccine trains the immune system to recognise a germ using a harmless piece or weakened form of it.

[Temperature | celsius fahrenheit kelvin freezing boiling]
Water freezes at 0 degrees Celsius (32 Fahrenheit) and boils at 100 Celsius (212 Fahrenheit) at sea level.
Absolute zero, the coldest possible temperature, is minus 273.15 Celsius or 0 kelvin.

[Newton's laws | motion inertia force]
Newton's three laws of motion: objects keep moving unless a force acts, force equals mass times acceleration, and every action has an equal and opposite reaction.

[Human body | bones heart organs skeleton]
An adult human body has 206 bones.
The heart has four chambers and beats roughly 100,000 times a day.

# ---------------- maths
[Pi | circle circumference]
Pi is the ratio of a circle's circumference to its diameter, about 3.14159. It is irrational, so its digits never repeat or end.

[Prime number | primes]
A prime number is a whole number greater than 1 that is divisible only by 1 and itself. 2 is the only even prime.
There are infinitely many primes, as Euclid proved around 300 BC.

[Pythagorean theorem | pythagoras triangle hypotenuse]
In a right triangle, the square of the hypotenuse equals the sum of the squares of the other two sides: a² + b² = c².

[Fibonacci sequence | fibonacci]
The Fibonacci sequence starts 0, 1, 1, 2, 3, 5, 8, 13 and each number is the sum of the two before it.

[Binary | base two bits]
Binary is base 2, using only the digits 0 and 1. Computers use it because a switch is either off or on. For example 1010 in binary is 10.

[Hexadecimal | hex base sixteen]
Hexadecimal is base 16, using the digits 0 to 9 and the letters A to F. Colours like #FF0000 are written in it.

[Big O notation | complexity runtime]
Big O notation describes how an algorithm's running time grows with the size of its input, for example O(1), O(log n), O(n) and O(n²).

# ---------------- computing
[Computer | pc hardware]
A computer is a machine that follows instructions to process data. It is made of a processor, memory, storage and input and output devices.

[CPU | processor cores]
The CPU, or processor, executes a program's instructions. Modern ones have several cores and run at billions of cycles per second.

[RAM | memory]
RAM is the fast working memory a computer uses for running programs. Its contents are lost when the power goes off.

[Internet | network tcp ip]
The internet is the global network of networks that computers use to talk to each other using protocols like TCP/IP.
The web is just one service that runs on it.

[World Wide Web | web www browser http berners-lee]
The World Wide Web is a system of linked pages served over HTTP and read in browsers. It was invented by Tim Berners-Lee at CERN in 1989.

[Artificial intelligence | ai]
Artificial intelligence is software that does tasks that normally need human intelligence, like recognising speech, images or language.

[Machine learning | training data model]
Machine learning is a way of building software that learns patterns from examples instead of being programmed with explicit rules.

[Neural network | neurons layers weights]
A neural network is a stack of layers of simple units whose weights are tuned from data using backpropagation and gradient descent.
I am one, a very small one: a few hundred thousand weights that route your message to an intent.

[Gradient descent | backpropagation backprop optimizer loss]
Gradient descent trains a model by repeatedly nudging each weight in the direction that lowers the loss.
Backpropagation is the chain-rule calculation that tells it which direction that is.

[Large language model | llm chatgpt claude gpt gemini]
A large language model is a neural network, usually a transformer, trained on huge amounts of text to predict the next word. Chat assistants like ChatGPT and Claude are built on them.
I am not one. I route your message to an intent, run a few skills and look things up in a small knowledge base, so I cannot write new text the way they do.

[Transformer | attention]
The transformer is a neural network design built around attention, introduced in 2017. It is the basis of modern language models.

[Operating system | os windows macos android]
An operating system manages a computer's hardware and runs programs for you. Examples are Windows, macOS, Linux and Android.

[Linux | tux torvalds]
Linux is a free, open-source operating system kernel created by Linus Torvalds in 1991. It runs most servers and Android phones.

[Kernel | syscall]
A kernel is the core of an operating system. It schedules programs, manages memory and talks to hardware.

[Compiler | compile parser]
A compiler translates source code into machine code or another language. Typical stages are lexing, parsing, optimising and code generation.

[Git | version control github]
Git is a distributed version control system that tracks changes to files. Linus Torvalds created it in 2005.

[Rust programming language | rustc cargo borrow checker ownership]
Rust is a systems programming language that guarantees memory safety without a garbage collector, using ownership and borrowing.
Its first stable release was in 2015 and its build tool is called Cargo.

[Python programming language | python]
Python is a readable, high-level programming language created by Guido van Rossum in 1991. It is popular for scripting, data science and AI.

[C programming language | clang]
C is a low-level programming language created by Dennis Ritchie at Bell Labs in the early 1970s. Unix was written in it.

[JavaScript | js node browser]
JavaScript is the programming language of web browsers, and with Node.js it also runs on servers.

[API | interface endpoint]
An API is an interface that lets one program use another's features, for example a web service with documented endpoints.

[Database | sql tables]
A database stores organised data so it can be searched and updated quickly. Many use SQL, a language for querying tables.

[Encryption | cryptography cipher aes rsa https]
Encryption scrambles data so that only someone with the right key can read it. HTTPS, AES and RSA are common examples.

[Bit and byte | bit byte kilobyte]
A bit is a single 0 or 1. A byte is 8 bits.

[Hash function | hash checksum]
A hash function turns any input into a fixed-size number. It is used in hash tables, checksums and passwords. I use hashing to turn words into feature ids.

[Algorithm | procedure]
An algorithm is a step-by-step procedure for solving a problem, like a recipe that a computer can follow.

[Recursion | recursive]
Recursion is when a function solves a problem by calling itself on a smaller piece of it, with a base case that stops the calls.

[Open source | license]
Open source software publishes its code under a licence that lets anyone read, use and change it.

[Blockchain | bitcoin crypto ledger]
A blockchain is a shared ledger of blocks linked by hashes so past entries cannot be changed quietly. Bitcoin, launched in 2009, was the first big one.

[Cloud computing | cloud servers aws]
Cloud computing means renting servers, storage and services over the internet instead of owning the hardware.

It is named after the astronomer Edwin Hubble.

# ---------------- science and nature
[Speed of sound | mach supersonic]
Sound travels through air at about 343 metres per second (roughly 1,235 km/h) at 20 degrees Celsius.
It is much faster in water and in solids, and it cannot travel through a vacuum.

[Absolute zero | kelvin]
Absolute zero is the lowest possible temperature: 0 kelvin, which is -273.15 degrees Celsius.
At that point particles have the least motion that physics allows.

[Oxygen | nitrogen]
Oxygen makes up about 21 percent of the air we breathe. Nitrogen makes up about 78 percent.
Animals use oxygen to release energy from food, and plants release it during photosynthesis.

[Carbon dioxide | co2 greenhouse gas]
Carbon dioxide, CO2, is a gas that makes up about 0.04 percent of the air.
Plants take it in for photosynthesis, animals breathe it out, and burning fossil fuels adds more of it, which traps heat.

[Dinosaurs | dinosaur extinction asteroid fossils t rex]
Dinosaurs appeared roughly 230 million years ago and ruled the land for well over 150 million years.
Most of them died out about 66 million years ago, after an asteroid hit Earth. Birds are their living descendants.

[Blue whale | whale krill]
The blue whale is the largest animal known to have ever lived, reaching about 30 metres in length.
Its heart is the size of a small car, and it eats tiny shrimp-like animals called krill.

[Cheetah | cheetahs]
The cheetah is the fastest land animal, reaching about 100 km/h in short sprints.
It can only keep that speed for a few hundred metres.

[Mariana Trench | challenger]
The Mariana Trench in the western Pacific Ocean is the deepest known part of the ocean.
Its deepest point, Challenger Deep, is almost 11 kilometres below the surface.

[Tectonic plates | earthquake earthquakes volcano plate]
Earth's outer shell is broken into large moving pieces called tectonic plates, which drift a few centimetres a year.
Most earthquakes and volcanoes happen where plates meet.

[Eclipse | solar eclipse lunar eclipse]
A solar eclipse happens when the Moon passes between the Sun and Earth and blocks the Sun's light.
A lunar eclipse happens when Earth passes between the Sun and the Moon, so Earth's shadow falls on the Moon.

[Rainbow | rainbows spectrum]
A rainbow forms when sunlight is bent and reflected inside raindrops, which splits it into its colours.
The colours run from red on the outside to violet on the inside.

[Blood | plasma]
Blood carries oxygen and nutrients around the body. Red blood cells carry the oxygen.
An adult has about 5 litres of blood.

[Brain | neurons neuron]
The human brain has about 86 billion neurons, which talk to each other with electrical and chemical signals.
It uses about a fifth of the body's energy while weighing only around 1.4 kilograms.

[Heart | heartbeat pulse]
The human heart is a muscle that pumps blood around the body, and it beats about 100,000 times a day.
It has four chambers: two atria and two ventricles.

# ---------------- maths
[Golden ratio | phi 1.618]
The golden ratio is about 1.618, often written with the Greek letter phi.
It is the limit of the ratio between neighbouring numbers in the Fibonacci sequence.

[Euler's constant | euler 2.718 natural logarithm]
Euler's number, written e, is about 2.71828. It is the base of the natural logarithm and shows up in growth and decay.

[Factorial | factorials]
The factorial of a number n, written n!, is n multiplied by every whole number below it down to 1.
For example 5! is 120, and 0! is defined as 1.

[Mean median and mode | average statistics]
The mean is the sum of the numbers divided by how many there are. The median is the middle value when they are sorted.
The mode is the value that appears most often.

# ---------------- computing
[DNS | domain name system domain names]
DNS, the Domain Name System, is the internet's phone book: it turns names like example.com into the IP addresses computers use.

[IP address | ipv4 ipv6 internet protocol address]
An IP address is the number that identifies a device on a network. IPv4 addresses are four numbers from 0 to 255, such as 192.168.0.1.
IPv6 addresses are 128 bits long, which gives far more of them.

[SQL | structured query language relational]
SQL, Structured Query Language, is the language used to ask relational databases for data.
A query like SELECT name FROM users WHERE age > 18 picks rows from a table.

[HTML and CSS | html css styling]
HTML describes the structure of a web page: headings, paragraphs, links and images.
CSS describes how it looks: colours, fonts and layout.

[GPU | graphics card graphics processing unit]
A GPU, graphics processing unit, has thousands of small cores that do many simple calculations at once.
It was built for graphics, and it is also what most neural networks are trained on.

[SSD | solid state hdd]
An SSD, solid-state drive, stores data in flash memory with no moving parts.
It is much faster than a spinning hard disk drive.

[Binary search | halving]
Binary search finds an item in a sorted list by checking the middle and discarding half each time, so it takes about log2(n) steps.
With 100 possibilities it never takes more than 7 checks, because 2 to the power 7 is 128.

[Sorting algorithm | quicksort mergesort sort]
A sorting algorithm puts items in order. Merge sort and quicksort take about n log n steps on average, while simple methods like bubble sort take n squared.

[Hash table | hashmap]
A hash table stores key and value pairs and finds a key in about one step on average by turning it into an array position with a hash function.

[Stack and queue | lifo fifo]
A stack is last in, first out, like a pile of plates. A queue is first in, first out, like people waiting in line.

[Unicode and UTF-8 | unicode utf8 emoji encoding]
Unicode gives every character, from letters to emoji, its own number, and there are over 150,000 of them.
UTF-8 stores those numbers in 1 to 4 bytes, and the first 128 match ASCII.

[ASCII | ascii]
ASCII is an old character code with 128 entries that fit in 7 bits. For example, capital A is 65 and lowercase a is 97.

[Alan Turing | turing machine enigma bletchley]
Alan Turing was a British mathematician who described the Turing machine in 1936, the idea behind modern computers.
During World War II he helped break the German Enigma code at Bletchley Park.

[Ada Lovelace | lovelace babbage]
Ada Lovelace was an English mathematician who wrote the first published computer algorithm, in 1843, for Charles Babbage's Analytical Engine.
She is often called the first programmer.

[Moore's law | transistors doubling]
Moore's law is the observation, made by Gordon Moore in 1965, that the number of transistors on a chip doubles roughly every two years.

[Bluetooth | wireless]
Bluetooth is a short-range wireless standard. It is named after Harald Bluetooth, a 10th century Danish king who united tribes, as the technology aims to unite devices.

# ---------------- history and culture
[Cold War | cold war ussr soviet union]
The Cold War was the long rivalry between the United States and the Soviet Union, from about 1947 until the Soviet Union ended in 1991.
It never became a direct war between the two, but it drove the arms race and the space race.

[Berlin Wall | berlin wall east germany]
The Berlin Wall was built in 1961 to separate East and West Berlin, and its fall came on 9 November 1989.
Germany reunified in October 1990.

[Renaissance | renaissance leonardo michelangelo]
The Renaissance was a period of art and learning in Europe from the 14th to the 17th century, which began in Italy.
Leonardo da Vinci and Michelangelo are two of its best known artists.

[Magna Carta | 1215 charter]
The Magna Carta was a charter signed by King John of England in 1215 that limited the king's power.
It is seen as an early step toward the rule of law.

[American independence | declaration of independence 4 july 1776]
The United States Declaration of Independence was adopted on 4 July 1776, announcing that the thirteen colonies were free of British rule.

[Great Wall of China | great wall wall of china]
The Great Wall of China is a series of walls built over many centuries, with a total length of more than 20,000 kilometres.
It is a myth that it can be seen from space with the naked eye.

[Olympic Games | olympics olympic]
The Olympic Games are held every four years. The modern Games began in Athens in 1896.
Paris hosted the 2024 Summer Games, and Los Angeles will host in 2028.

[FIFA World Cup | world cup football soccer]
The FIFA World Cup is the top football (soccer) tournament, held every four years. Argentina won in 2022.
The 2026 tournament was hosted jointly by the United States, Canada and Mexico.

[Cricket | odi t20 ipl]
Cricket is a bat and ball game between two teams of eleven players. The main formats are Test matches, one-day internationals and Twenty20.
India won the one-day World Cup in 1983 and 2011.

[Chess | chaturanga checkmate]
Chess is a board game for two on 64 squares, where each side starts with 16 pieces and the aim is to checkmate the other king.
It developed from chaturanga, a game played in India around the 6th century.
